Types of PDF forms you can fill out

Almost every PDF form falls into one of two categories. Understanding which type you have makes the whole process easier.

1. Fillable PDF forms (AcroForm)

These are PDFs with interactive form fields built in. When you click on the field, a cursor appears and you type. The fields typically have light blue highlights or visible boxes on the page.

Common examples:

  • IRS tax forms (W-9, W-4, 1040)
  • Government applications
  • Bank account opening forms
  • Insurance claim forms
  • Job application forms

2. Non-fillable PDF forms (flat PDFs)

These look identical to paper forms — blank lines and boxes but no interactive fields. They're typically scans, or PDFs exported from Word or Google Docs without form metadata.

Common examples:

  • Scanned rental applications
  • Photocopied contracts
  • Older government forms
  • School enrollment forms

Both types are supported by FillPDF.app — it just handles them differently.

How to fill out a PDF form online free — step by step

Step 1: Open FillPDF.app

Go to FillPDF.app. The site is free, requires no signup, and works in any modern browser. The page loads in under a second.

Step 2: Upload your form

Two ways to add your PDF:

  • Click Choose PDF and select the form from your device
  • Drag the file directly onto the upload box

The form loads immediately. Your file is processed in your browser and never uploaded to a server.

Step 3: Fill in the form fields

If the form has interactive fields, FillPDF.app detects them automatically and turns them into live inputs. You'll see them outlined on the page — just click or tap into each one and type.

Tips for filling form fields quickly:

  • Use the Tab button on the built-in keyboard to jump to the next empty field
  • For phone numbers and IDs, use the Number tool — it opens directly on a digits layout
  • For email addresses, use the Email tool — it keeps @ and . always visible
  • For dates, use the Date tool to insert today's date in one click

Step 4: Fill out any fields that aren't detected

Some forms have boxes that aren't tagged as form fields — signature lines, additional notes, or fill-in areas added later. For these, use the Text tool:

  1. Click Text in the toolbar
  2. Click on the form where the text should go
  3. Type your content
  4. Press Done on the keyboard

The text appears exactly where you placed it — even if the form doesn't formally recognize that area as fillable.

Step 5: Add checkmarks where needed

Many forms have yes/no questions, "check all that apply" sections, or option boxes. Use the Check tool:

  • Tap Check in the toolbar
  • Tap the box or area where the mark should go
  • A checkmark appears instantly

To switch between a checkmark (✓) and an X (✕), select the checkmark and tap the alternate symbol in the options bar.

Step 6: Review every field

Before downloading, scroll through the entire document and check:

  • Every required field is filled in
  • No typos in names, addresses or numbers
  • Dates are correct
  • Checkmarks are in the right boxes
  • The right options are selected

Use the Undo button to fix any mistakes, or tap Select and then Delete to remove individual items.

Step 7: Add your signature

Most forms require a signature. Here's how:

  1. Click Sign in the toolbar
  2. A signature pad opens in a modal window
  3. Draw your signature with your mouse, stylus or finger
  4. Click Add Signature
  5. Tap the page where the signature belongs

You can resize the signature by dragging the corner handle, or move it with the arrow pad in the options bar.

Step 8: Download the completed form

Click Download Filled PDF. The completed document is generated in your browser and saved to your device with -filled.pdf appended to the filename.

Your original form is not modified — you get a new file. Keep it for your records, or send it back to whoever asked for it.

Filling out common PDF forms — quick tips

Job applications

Job application PDFs often have a mix of fillable and non-fillable fields. Use the Tab key to move between detected fields quickly, and use the Text tool for any open-ended questions ("Why do you want this role?"). Sign at the bottom with the Sign tool.

Rental applications

Rental PDFs usually ask for employment history, references, and previous addresses. Add all of this with the Text tool if the fields aren't detected. Remember to sign the bottom — most landlords won't accept an unsigned application.

Tax forms (W-9, W-4, 1040)

IRS forms are almost always fillable PDFs. The form fields are detected automatically. Use the Number tool for SSN, EIN, and dollar amounts. Sign in the "Signature" field at the bottom with the Sign tool.

Medical intake forms

Medical forms ask for detailed personal and family history. Use the Text tool for paragraphs of information. Place checkmarks in the "Yes/No" boxes. Sign the consent section at the end.

School enrollment forms

These often require information about both the student and the parent. Fill in each section separately, and use the Date tool for the enrollment date and the parent's signature date.

Frequently asked questions

Can I fill out a PDF form without Adobe Acrobat?

Yes. FillPDF.app runs entirely in your browser. You never need Adobe Acrobat, Reader or any other desktop PDF software.

Can I save a partially completed form and finish it later?

The site doesn't have a "save draft" feature — but you can download the partially filled PDF, then re-upload it later to continue. Your content will be preserved.

Will the form look professional when I send it back?

Yes. The text renders in a clean sans-serif font at the exact position you place it. Checkmarks, signatures and dates all appear correctly. The result looks like it was filled in using desktop software.

Can I fill out a government form with this?

For most forms, yes. But some government agencies require specific digital signature standards (like PKI-based signatures) that this tool does not provide. For those, check the form's instructions before using any web-based filler.

Does it work on iPhone and Android?

Yes. FillPDF.app is designed for mobile-first use and includes its own in-browser keyboard. Filling forms on a phone works the same as on desktop — no fighting with the mobile OS keyboard.

Is my data safe?

Yes. Everything happens in your browser. Your form is never uploaded, stored or transmitted. When you close the tab, it's gone.
